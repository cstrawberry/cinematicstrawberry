The Principle of Physical Instantiation

Abstract

Wigner (1960) asked why the laws of physics follow precise mathematical rules. The Principle of Physical Instantiation (PPI) answers by making finite realizability the filter. A proposed abstract requirement acquires physical content only through a finite protocol realization with declared carriers, units, resource ledgers, and empirical readouts, so physical law is the resource-efficient embodiment of the logical and informational structures that survive that filter. This principle explains how these structures, when instantiated by systems with finite resources, are shaped by irreducible thermodynamic costs and resource-optimization imperatives, resulting in the emergence of physical laws as their most efficient embodiments. Four cases follow, each on its own stated branch. The arrow of time appears where a certified positive rate of entropy production accompanies the ordered predict-verify-update sequence; Quantum Mechanics appears on the complex Hilbert branch, where noncontextual additivity over operationally equivalent alternatives fixes the Born form, with the framework proving separately that self-reference alone does not force it; gauge structure appears as the efficient way to compare predictive states across a network; and Einstein's equation appears as an equation of state on the local thermal branch. Each result carries its conditions, and physical law is read as what logical structure becomes once finite realization filters it.

The Principle in Plain Terms

A blueprint for a bridge can specify a cable of zero thickness carrying infinite load. Nothing in geometry forbids it. What forbids it is steel: a real cable has a diameter, a weight, a price, and a breaking point, and those constraints select which of the drawable bridges get built. The drawings are not wrong. They are unfiltered.

This principle says physical law relates to logic the way built bridges relate to drawings. Logic and mathematics describe every structure that holds together. Only some of those structures can be carried by something finite that has to make records, pay for them, and act on them in time. A distinction that no finite procedure can register, at any cost, does not become part of physics. The reason is carriage rather than falsehood: nothing can hold it.

The filter has three parts, and each is a real cost. A distinction has to be readable by some finite protocol. Keeping and erasing a record takes energy, so memory is never free. And when two descriptions differ only in something no procedure can detect, they count as the same physical situation, and the cheaper one is kept.

Run the filter and several familiar laws look less arbitrary. Time has a direction because updating a record is not free and cannot be undone for nothing. Comparing states across a network needs a field that reconciles local conventions, which is what a gauge field does. Geometry cannot be a fixed backdrop when the things in it carry the cost of their own records. None of these is proved from logic alone. Each is what logic looks like after the filter, on a branch whose conditions are stated.

1. Introduction: The Effectiveness of Logic in Physics

From the elegant equations of electromagnetism to the complex geometry of spacetime, physical reality seems to operate according to a hidden logical and mathematical blueprint. But why should this be so? Why isn't the universe a chaotic, rule-less mess? Traditional physics takes these laws as given, treating them as fundamental features to be discovered rather than explained.

The Predictive Universe (PU) explains that this blueprint arises from a single, foundational requirement: for a universe to be knowable, it must be predictive. This necessity creates a set of abstract logical challenges. For instance, how can a system accurately predict its own future without getting trapped in a paradoxical infinite loop? The prediction must account for the act of predicting itself, creating an endless feedback loop. This is not yet a physical problem; it is a pure paradox of information and logic that any coherent universe must grapple with.

This is where the Principle of Physical Instantiation (PPI) provides the link. PPI states that abstract logical requirements gain physical content only through finite protocol-response records. Response-null labels are quotiented away, and resource-efficient representatives are selected by compression efficiency. Physics, in this view, is logic under finite-resource pressure.

2. Layer One: The Abstract Requirements of a Knowable Universe

The framework begins from the only irrefutable starting point for knowledge, ahead of matter or energy: the certainty of conscious awareness, as captured by Descartes' Cogito (Descartes 1996). The framework's core insight is to identify this process - the act of thinking, of doubting - as fundamentally predictive.

As explored in Boundaries of Meaningful Inquiry, knowledge requires predictors. A universe without predictors may be discussed from an external standpoint, but it contains no internal system for which descriptions become knowledge. From the requirements of predictive inquiry we can identify abstract constraints that must be physically instantiated by any knowable reality.

These are the logical scaffolding on which physical laws are built, and they are not themselves physical laws. The PPI focuses on how the universe resolves the tensions and challenges posed by these abstract requirements.

2.1 Foundational Logical Challenges

The PU framework identifies several key logical challenges that a predictive universe must solve. For example:

  1. The Challenge of Self-Reference: For a system to know itself, it must be able to model itself. The Self-Referential Paradox of Accurate Prediction (SPAP) proves that within a class of predictors rich enough to build a system acting against its own forecast, no predictor in that class is correct across the class. A forecast delivered to the system it describes becomes part of what is being forecast, and the demand that a finite description literally contain its own value cannot be met.

    • Abstract Requirement: The system must be self-referential to be knowable, yet it must have a built-in mechanism to avoid the logical self-destruction of this paradox.

  2. The Challenge of Causality: Prediction is an inherently causal process - it demands a stable relationship where causes precede effects. If information could flow backward in time to alter the events that created it (the essence of a grandfather paradox), the logical chain of `cause → predict → effect` would collapse. The past would be unstable, and the universe would become an unknowable, self-contradictory chaos.

    • Abstract Requirement: The system must enforce a strict, one-way causal structure, ensuring that effects cannot retroactively alter their causes.

These are not physical problems yet; they are paradoxes and challenges at the level of pure logic and information. The Principle of Physical Instantiation describes how they get solved physically.

3. Layer Two: The Principle of Physical Instantiation in Action

  • Abstract Structures: The set of self-consistent abstract objects, structures, and systems that are logically derivable or constructible from the foundational axiomatic system established by the predictive cycle. At this level, definitions are not yet constrained by physical resources.
  • Physical Instantiation: A derivable, self-consistent logical or mathematical structure becomes physically relevant only when finite resources, response records, and protocol access carry it. PPI filters mathematical structures through thermodynamic cost, compression efficiency, and predictive stability before they count as physical.

This embodiment process is not free. It is governed by thermodynamics and a drive for maximum efficiency, which the PU framework formalizes as the Principle of Compression Efficiency (PCE). Candidate physical laws are the efficient solutions that survive this filtering process.

The Principle of Physical Instantiation (PPI) asserts that physical laws are the signature of this constrained instantiation process.

3.1 The Arrow of Time: The Physical Enforcement of Predictive Logic

How does the universe ensure that time flows in a consistent, forward direction?

  • The Logical Requirement: As established in the PU explanation of Time's Arrow, the very act of prediction is logically irreversible. The sequence `Predict → Verify → Update` imposes a fundamental, directional flow on the elementary processes of reality. This is a logical necessity for any knowable universe.
  • The Physical Cost and Enforcement: The logical update must be physically instantiated. The `Verify/Update` step is realized by the `Evolve` process, which retains a binary verification record of structural size ε0 = ln 2. Physical reset cost is accounted on a separate ledger, εphys = Hq(P | R) + εdiss ≥ Hq(P | R), with dissipation depending on the substrate and the floor for an unbiased record set by Landauer (1961). On the branch carrying a positive pathwise entropy-production certificate, repeated update supports the macroscopic irreversibility of predictive updating.
  • The Emergent Law: The irreversible arrow of time is the resulting physical law. It is the law of predictive causality, held in place on the branch where a common forward and reverse event algebra carries a certified positive rate of entropy production.

3.2 Quantum Mechanics: The Physical Response to Self-Reference

How does the universe tackle the SPAP paradox?

  • The Branch: On the branch that supplies a stochastic update law with no retained deterministic refinement, unresolved alternatives are represented by superposition in a complex Hilbert space, and quotienting the labels that make no difference to any response yields noncontextual additivity, which fixes the Born form through the Gleason (1957) and Busch (2003) representation. The framework proves separately that self-reference and compression alone do not force this branch, so the stochastic law is an input rather than a consequence.
  • The Physical Cost: Closing the self-referential loop with finite memory is a logically irreversible act. It retains a verification record of structural size ε0 = ln 2, and any physical implementation satisfies εphys = Hq(P | R) + εdiss ≥ Hq(P | R) on its registered reset branch. This accounting marks finite update and contributes to the irreversibility of quantum measurement.
  • The Emergent Law: Quantum Mechanics is the resulting physical theory. It is the representation a finite predictive substrate takes on that branch, and it is the machinery a universe needs in order to be self-referential without contradiction.

3.3 Gauge Theory: The Physical Solution to Coherence

How does the universe efficiently solve the problem of comparing predictive states across the network?

  • The Solution: The most resource-efficient solution is not to track every relative phase explicitly - which would be like trying to perfectly synchronize millions of tiny, independent clocks - but to introduce a new dynamical field - a connection field, or gauge field - that compensates for local phase changes. This field acts like a local time-zone adjuster, allowing any two 'clocks' to be compared meaningfully without needing to know their absolute setting.
  • The Physical Cost: This new field has its own energy and complexity, which contributes to the overall cost budget. The system's drive for efficiency ensures the dynamics of this field are as simple and low-cost as possible while still doing its job.
  • The Emergent Law: On the gauge-coherence branch, the simplest efficient dynamics for this connection field are described by gauge theory, such as Maxwell-type equations for electromagnetism. It is the law of coherence under information-bandwidth constraints.

3.4 General Relativity: The Physical Solution to the Background Contradiction

How does the universe reconcile being both the actor and the stage?

  • The Solution: The system abandons the idea of a static, absolute background. Instead, it enforces a principle of local consistency. The "stage" (geometry) must be in continuous thermodynamic equilibrium with the "actors" (the predictive activity of the MPU network, quantified by its stress-energy tensor).
  • The Physical Cost: This consistency is enforced by the laws of thermodynamics applied to causal boundaries (horizons). The information capacity of these boundaries is limited, in the form Bekenstein (1973) introduced and Jacobson (1995) turned into a derivation of the field equations from local thermodynamics, which follows from the channel-support bound on MPU interactions, Cmax ≤ ln d0 − ln 2, a statement about retained channel support rather than about dissipated heat.
  • The Emergent Law: On the emergent-gravity branch, local horizon thermodynamics links geometry to energy and momentum. The resulting effective description has the form of Einstein's field equations. General Relativity is the law of geometry under local thermodynamic-equilibrium constraints.

4. Conclusion

The Principle of Physical Instantiation makes finite realizability the filter between logical structure and physical law. On this reading the laws of physics are the structures that survive it, shaped by resource efficiency and by the cost of records, and each of the four cases above holds on conditions that are stated rather than assumed.

In this view, physical law is the stable finite-response structure selected under resource constraints. The laws of nature are the indelible signatures of this ongoing process: the physical embodiment of a reality built on prediction.
