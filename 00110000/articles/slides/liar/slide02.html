The Classical Derivation

Four stated principles and one unstated premise

What it says it uses

  • Bivalence. Every statement is true or false.
  • Truth schema. "X is true" is true exactly when X is true.
  • Self-reference. Statements may refer to themselves.
  • Classical logic. Excluded middle, non-contradiction, ordinary inference.

What it also uses

Occurrence identity. The sentence that evaluates and the sentence that is evaluated carry one value at one position.

τ(L) = ¬τ(L)

The equation has one argument, the expression, and it is written only after the two occurrences have been identified.

The truth schema bundles two principles: disquotation, which is kept, and occurrence identity, which the liar's own content refutes.

The Universality Assumption: one value for the expression across both of its positions
